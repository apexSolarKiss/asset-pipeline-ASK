## What Past Campaigns Are

Past campaigns are the brand's *historical execution evidence* — the campaign-shaped work the brand has run, including the campaign-world articulated, the creative concept and arc pursued, the asset families produced, the channels reached, the messages carried (where present), the governance trace recorded (where preserved), the assets that became references for later work, and where preservable the negative evidence of what was rejected, killed, or retired.

The single load-bearing framing: **past campaigns are evidence of what the brand has done; they are not authority over what the brand should do next**. Where current articulation supersedes past-campaign content (a re-platformed brand; a refreshed visual system; a strategic pivot away from a historical posture), past campaigns retain continuity-reference value but lose authority. Where current articulation is sparse or absent and past campaigns demonstrate sustained pattern, past campaigns may carry authority by default — but the load-bearing relationship is one of demonstrated history, not articulated direction. Intake must distinguish the two cases at every point of resolution.

### Distinction from the other five input categories

- **Brand platform** articulates *what the brand is* (values, voice, stance, audience, promise, authority, narrative themes, exclusion boundaries) — propositional identity. Past campaigns demonstrate *how the brand has executed* against that identity. Brand platform is forward-looking and prescriptive; past campaigns are backward-looking and demonstrative.
- **Visual identity system** articulates brand-wide visual *constants* (logo, typography, color codes, grid systems) plus their application guides. Past campaigns demonstrate the constants in execution — and frequently surface execution extensions or deviations the VIS articulation does not explicitly name (a campaign that bent typography hierarchy; a campaign that introduced a one-off color extension).
- **Photography / image style guide** articulates conventions for how the brand photographs its subject. Past campaigns demonstrate those conventions in operation across multiple campaign worlds — and frequently demonstrate convention variation across campaigns under the same brand (the brand's photographic register may shift per-campaign within tolerance the style guide does not articulate).
- **Asset library** is the brand's accumulated approved *imagery as population* — a flat or thinly-organized collection of approved visual work. Past campaigns are the *campaign-as-execution-unit* of which imagery is one element among campaign-world, arc, channel-propagation, governance, and outcomes. The categories overlap at the imagery layer (asset library's "prior campaign imagery" type per [`docs/brand-system-input-asset-library-to-ia-mapping-v1.md`](brand-system-input-asset-library-to-ia-mapping-v1.md) draws from past-campaigns content); past campaigns carry the *execution-unit envelope* asset library does not — what arc the imagery belonged to, what concept it expressed, what channels it reached, what governance shaped it, what came before and what followed.
- **Application guidelines** articulate *how* the brand intends to apply itself across surfaces — the rule layer. Past campaigns demonstrate the application in operation across multiple campaigns — and frequently surface application-rule variation per-campaign (a campaign that operated in touchpoints application guidelines do not name; a campaign that adapted brand-identifier presence under campaign-world pressure).

The boundary running across all five distinctions: past campaigns are the *historical record layer* of the brand system. The other five categories define what the brand is (platform), how it formally appears (VIS), how it photographs (style guide), what imagery it has approved (asset library), or how it applies itself across surfaces (application guidelines). Past campaigns demonstrate all of those in execution across time, with the temporal dimension as their distinguishing feature.

### What counts as past-campaigns input

Past-campaigns content typically articulates the brand's historical execution across nine to ten components. Each is conceptually distinct even when a brand preserves several together or none in structured form at all.

#### Campaign-world content

The visual world a campaign established — its setting, atmosphere, register, ambient logic. The campaign-world content per past campaign is what makes that campaign feel like its own bounded world rather than generic brand work. PKT-CAMP-004's Warm Restraint campaign-world is the project's own most-deepened example of this content shape.

#### Creative concept / editorial arc

The campaign's organizing idea: what the campaign was *about* beyond its visuals; what story or argument it advanced; what arc moved across its moments. Where the brand preserves concept articulation (creative briefs; campaign book; agency briefs; strategic memos), this content is articulated. Where the brand preserves only the executed work, concept content must be inferred from the work's collective pattern.

#### Channel propagation patterns

How the campaign moved across channels — which channel was the anchor (typically print, premium digital, or hero film); which channels carried derivatives; what adaptations occurred per channel; what cross-channel coherence was maintained vs deliberately broken. Channel propagation traces overlap with application-guidelines content at brand-wide scope but are campaign-specific in execution.

#### Asset families per campaign

The set of assets produced for the campaign as a coherent family: campaign heroes, supporting visuals, derivative cuts, channel-specific adaptations. Family-coherence patterns within a campaign — what made the assets feel like one family rather than several — are demonstrable from preserved campaign work.

#### Message / offer / communication content

Where the campaign carried a message (a promotional offer; a brand-identity message; a launch announcement; a values statement), the message content the campaign articulated and the channel-specific message variants it produced. Many campaigns (particularly editorial / brand campaigns) carry minimal explicit message; this component varies in load.

#### Review / approval / governance pattern

The governance trace the campaign left: who approved what, when, against what criteria; what review boards convened; what override decisions occurred; what was raised at sign-off and resolved or escalated. Most brands preserve only final-approval state (the assets that shipped) without preserving the in-process governance chain that produced them.

#### Reuse / derivative / continuity-reference patterns

Which campaign assets became references for later work — campaign heroes lifted as brand-voice references for subsequent marketing packets; campaign-world references reused across an extended arc; specific governed outputs that became continuity anchors for derivative campaigns. Reuse patterns surface the brand's operational treatment of past-campaign assets as forward-looking inputs.

#### Temporal context

When the campaign ran; how long it remained active; what preceded and followed it; what events caused its retirement (campaign concluded; superseded by next campaign; pulled for strategic reasons; quietly retired without explicit retirement). Temporal context is what distinguishes current from historical from superseded campaign content — the load-bearing dimension for the historical-evidence-vs-authority distinction.

#### Negative evidence (where preserved)

Concepts rejected before production; campaigns killed mid-flight; arcs abandoned without execution; assets that did not survive review; campaigns retired early for performance or strategic reasons. Negative evidence is the most fragile preserved content in past-campaigns intake — brands frequently preserve only what shipped, with rejection and abandonment traces lost over time.

#### Outcomes / retrospective signals (where preserved)

Performance signals against campaign objectives (engagement; sentiment; market response; sales lift; brand-perception shift); retrospective evaluations by the brand or its agencies; "what we learned" notes; subsequent strategic adjustments traceable to the campaign's outcome. Like negative evidence, outcomes are frequently not preserved — and where preserved, are often partial or commercial-confidential to a degree intake cannot fully consume.

The nine-to-ten components are conceptually distinct but preserved asymmetrically in practice. Most brands preserve campaign-world content, asset families, and reuse patterns reliably; governance trace, negative evidence, and outcomes are preserved partially or not at all in many archives. Intake addresses each component for what the archive carries; gaps in archive completeness are themselves evidence about how the brand treats its own history.

## What Past Campaigns Contribute

The IA receives past-campaigns content as: campaign-world continuity evidence; creative concept / editorial arc evidence; family-coherence patterns across asset sets; channel adaptation execution evidence; message / offer / communication patterns in execution; review / approval / governance pattern evidence (where preserved); reuse / derivative / continuity-reference patterns; evidence of brand evolution or drift across campaigns; negative evidence about what failed, was rejected, retired, or superseded (where preserved); and outcomes / retrospective signals (where preserved).

Past campaigns provide content the other input categories do not: brand platform articulates intent without execution evidence; VIS articulates constants without showing them at campaign scale; photography style guide articulates conventions without showing them under campaign-world pressure; asset library shows imagery without the execution envelope around it; application guidelines articulate touchpoint rules without showing the cross-channel propagation those rules produced in operation. Past-campaigns content is the execution-unit-shaped evidence layer that sits between the other five categories' definitions / articulations and the IA's per-packet production work.

Three named limits on the contribution:

- **Past campaigns do not author current packet-level content.** They demonstrate what the brand has executed. Whether a past campaign's pattern should carry into a new packet's authorship is a separate decision — the operator's judgment about whether the pattern is current authority or historical reference, informed by the temporal-context content above.
- **Past campaigns are not brand doctrine by default.** They are evidence. Doctrine sits in brand platform, VIS, photography style guide, and application guidelines. Past campaigns may validate doctrine, contradict it, supersede it, or pressure it — but they do not themselves constitute doctrine. The architectural posture: past campaigns inform doctrine; doctrine retains its location in the articulation-based input categories.
- **Past campaigns can be lossy about their own logic.** Executed work demonstrates outcome without always demonstrating intent. A campaign's preserved imagery may show *what* the campaign was visually without preserving *why* — what concept it was advancing, what audience it was reaching, what strategic objective it served. Intake must distinguish preserved execution evidence from inferred-from-execution intent reconstruction.

## IA Layer Mapping

Past-campaigns content distributes across IA layers in a distinctive pattern: substantial contribution at the brand-system layer (campaign continuity evidence shaping brand-wide posture); concentrated at the mode-specific layer (especially campaign-mode); meaningful contribution at packet, slot, and curation-seam layers as historical templates and reference precedents; and the only input category that contributes structurally-recognizable content at the governed-asset layer via the governed-output continuity reference function.

### Brand-system layer (currently unmodeled in IA)

**Receives.** Cross-campaign continuity evidence — what the brand has consistently expressed across multiple campaigns; voice continuity demonstrated across executed work; visual register stability or evolution across campaigns; evidence of brand-system content the articulation-based input categories may not have surfaced (a recurring narrative motif present in three campaigns but not articulated in brand platform; a photographic posture sustained across campaigns but not codified in style guide).

**Resolution shape.** The brand-system layer has no first-class carrier (per [`docs/architecture.md`](architecture.md)'s Brand-System Layer Status named decision; three live shapes; hybrid most likely on current evidence). Past-campaigns content at this layer currently flows downstream as operator-interpretive guidance into prose carriers, the same way brand platform abstractions, VIS constants beyond approved references, photography style guide brand-level conventions, and application-guidelines cross-channel coherence principles do. The cross-campaign continuity evidence is content for the unmodeled-but-named layer; this deepening does not resolve the structural decision.

### Category / product-class layer (currently signaled-only in IA)

**Receives.** Per-category campaign-execution evidence: which categories past campaigns have featured; how category-specific imagery was treated within campaign worlds; whether category-typical photography conventions held under campaign pressure or got bent for campaign reasons; what campaign-world adaptations occurred per category. Most brands' campaign archives are organized by campaign (not by category) so category-specific past-campaign extraction is interpretive.

**Resolution shape.** Category presence is signaled-only in current IA. Category-specific past-campaigns content (when extractable) flows into per-category operator practice and into category-typical reference precedents; first-class category-aesthetic carriers remain unearned per Apex Named Limitation #1.

### Mode-specific layer (varies per mode)

**Receives.** The heaviest past-campaigns contribution at this layer — and the layer most asymmetrically loaded across the four modes.

- **Brand campaign / editorial mode** receives the most: each past campaign is a mode-specific data point that supplies campaign-world content, creative-concept content, editorial-arc patterns, and review-context conventions for the review-context / campaign-world coherence layer. The Phase 1 campaign-mode sketch's evidence base is essentially this content shape; past campaigns ARE the mode's primary evidence.
- **Marketing / message-driven mode** receives channel-adaptation patterns from past promotional campaigns (where preserved); historical message / offer content as message-archive evidence; cross-touchpoint message variant patterns.
- **Collection / merchandising mode** receives historical collection / merchandising patterns where past campaigns included merchandising-shaped moments; many brands' past campaigns blur collection mode into campaign mode (a campaign featuring a curated collection edit), making clean extraction interpretive.
- **SKU-driven mode** receives the least direct contribution; past campaigns supply historical product-photography precedent but not mode-specific overlay content for SKU mode (which has none).

**Resolution shape.** Each non-SKU mode carries at least one mode-specific layer. Past-campaigns content populates that layer's content asymmetrically across the four modes — heavily for campaign mode, moderately for marketing and collection modes, lightly for SKU mode. Carriers vary per mode; held composite-anchor candidates (`messages` / `briefs` for marketing; `campaign_concept` for campaign) are paper-pressed and would fit past-campaigns content if earned, but remain held.

### Packet layer (the strongest current carrier)

**Receives.** Historical packet-template content: recurring packet shapes the brand has produced across campaigns (e.g., "every campaign launch produces a hero plus three editorial supports plus a per-channel set" — historically demonstrated); packet-level reference precedents (specific past-campaign assets that became packet-level references for derivative work, like REF-002's cross-packet reuse across PKT-SKU-009 / PKT-SKU-010 / PKT-COLL-001 in the Phase 1 evidence — a paper-pressed continuity-reference pattern); packet-level `business_intent` and `creative_intent` archival content informing new packet authorship.

**Resolution shape.** Packet layer carries structurally explicit fields. Past-campaigns content flows into `approved_reference_keys` (when a past-campaign asset is elevated to current reference status), `creative_intent` text (historical-pattern-informed authorship), `bounded_creative_discretion` text (where historical execution bounds the discretion zone for similar new work), and `constraint_rules` (when a past-campaign approval pattern is operationalized as a packet-level constraint). The reuse / derivative governance question — under what conditions a past-campaign asset becomes a current packet-level reference — sits here; per the apex artifact, it is an adjacent not-yet-hardenable concern.

### Slot layer (the role-specific narrowing carrier)

**Receives.** Per-role precedent imagery from past campaigns — which slot roles past campaigns have produced and what role-typical compositions executed at scale; per-touchpoint per-role adaptations historically used; slot-level reference precedents where past-campaign assets serve as slot-specific anchors.

**Resolution shape.** `slot_role` enum carries role identity; `slot_prompt` text carries role-specific composition instructions including historical-pattern-informed direction; packet-level reference imagery surfaces into slot inputs via lookup, and where past-campaign assets sit in `reference_assets` they flow through the existing lookup chain. Slot-specific reference attachment as a first-class carrier remains unearned per Phase 1's held candidates.

### Candidate generation layer

**Receives.** Generally nothing directly. Candidate generation is transient by design; past-campaigns content bounds the layer's operation upstream (via slot_prompt, packet references, bounded creative discretion text) and downstream (via curation criteria).

**Resolution shape.** Past-campaigns content does not flow into the candidate-generation layer structurally; it shapes the layer's inputs and outputs from adjacent layers.

### Curation seam

**Receives.** Historical governance-pattern evidence: how past campaigns were curated (which curation patterns the brand has historically practiced); what review-context conventions applied; what governance authority chains were exercised; what override decisions occurred under campaign-world pressure; what selection criteria the brand has historically prioritized. Plus: per-campaign judgment-criteria precedent for current curation events drawing on similar past-campaign content.

**Resolution shape.** The curation seam carries clean structural carriers (five-axis provenance + `curator` + `curation_pattern` for candidate selection; `governed_output_status` + `governed_output_flag` + `asset_attachment` + `slot_status` lifecycle for governance-bearing curation). Past-campaigns content flows into curation criteria as historical-precedent content informing current judgment — `capture_reason` / `capture_notes` text may reference past-campaign patterns. The `curation_pattern` enum's five values (per Path A finding + collection-mode operational test) are mode-independent; past-campaigns content does not earn a new pattern value.

### Governed asset layer

**Receives.** The most distinctive past-campaigns contribution at any layer. Past campaigns supply the *governed-output continuity reference* — past-campaign governed assets that become upstream inputs for derivative packets. Per PR #239's ninth reference function: a campaign hero shot lifted as brand-voice reference for a later marketing packet; a governed SKU hero reused as a category reference; a campaign-world asset reused across an extended arc. Past-campaigns content is the *only* input category that contributes structurally-recognizable content at this layer beyond the architecture's downstream-of-curation default.

**Resolution shape.** Governed-output continuity reference is currently unmodeled at first-class depth. The architecture treats `reference_assets` as independently linkable, so a past-campaign governed output *can* be linked as a reference for new work — but the derivative-governance question (what governance applies to the derivative; when reuse becomes an authorized operation vs requiring fresh approval; how the brand's articulated reuse rules from application-guidelines content interact with the past-campaign asset's original approval scope) is held across all four modes per the apex artifact. Past-campaigns intake provides the source material for this layer's content; the layer's governance shape remains held.

## Relationship to PR #241 Extraction Sequence

PR #241 named past campaigns as Stage 5 // Validation of the seven-stage extraction sequence (`evidence → articulated conventions → abstraction → application → validation → defaults → inference/authorship`). This deepening reads against that placement.

### Stage 5 primary occupation

Past-campaigns content is what Stage 5 produces: cross-validation of Stages 1–4 against the brand's accumulated execution evidence; identification of mode activation against historical operating reality; detection of where stated articulation diverges from demonstrated practice; surfacing of evolution-not-inconsistency where newer campaigns show the brand moving in a direction older articulation does not name.

### Pressure on earlier stages

- **Stage 1 (evidence).** Past-campaigns content validates the asset library's completeness and organization. Where past campaigns are documented in the brand's records but their assets are missing from the asset library, the library is incomplete relative to the brand's history (a recovery opportunity for intake). Where the asset library contains imagery that no documented campaign accounts for, the campaign-archive coverage is incomplete (the inverse). Per the visual-as-fidelity-strategy claim from the apex aesthetic-layering pass, the asset library carries when conflict surfaces; past campaigns help intake identify where the library is and is not complete relative to documented history.
- **Stage 2 (articulated conventions).** Past-campaigns content validates whether photography style guide articulated conventions hold in execution. A brand's style guide says "use generous side-light"; past campaigns demonstrate whether that convention held across campaign-world variations or was bent at scale. Where they diverge, executed work carries per visual-as-fidelity-strategy — the convention has either evolved or been performative.
- **Stage 3 (abstraction).** Past-campaigns content validates brand-platform abstractions against demonstrated brand behavior. A brand articulates "warm but not sentimental"; past campaigns demonstrate whether executed work read warm or cool, sentimental or restrained. The articulated-vs-demonstrated cross-validation surfaces where brand platform articulation is operational versus aspirational (per the brand platform deepening's "most likely to be ghostwritten or aspirational" observation).
- **Stage 4 (application guidelines).** Past-campaigns content validates the stated mode-activation and touchpoint-set. A brand's application guidelines name campaign mode as active; past campaigns demonstrate whether the brand has actually run campaigns. The reverse case: application guidelines do not name a mode that past campaigns demonstrate the brand operating in (a brand whose application guidelines omit marketing / message-driven mode but whose past campaigns include extensive promotional touchpoint adaptations). The cross-pressure surfaces stated-vs-operating divergence the operator must resolve.

### Validation conditions

Four distinct conditions shape Stage 5's validation behavior:

- **Past campaigns absent.** Pre-launch brand; new-launch brand without campaign history; lapsed campaign program. Stage 5 cannot validate; the architecture relies on Stages 1–4 only. Operational pressure becomes the deferred validation mechanism — when the brand begins running campaigns, those first campaigns become Stage 5 content for future intake cycles. Architecturally: the architecture functions normally; the validation surface is unpopulated.
- **Past campaigns stale.** The brand has campaign history but it predates significant articulation changes (re-platforming; visual identity refresh; strategic pivot). Stage 5 produces evidence that may no longer reflect current brand posture. The operator marks past-campaign content as historical-not-current; uses it for continuity reference but not authority. Architecturally: validation is partial; intake distinguishes stale evidence from current evidence at the temporal-context dimension.
- **Past campaigns divergent.** Campaign archive shows multiple stylistic eras (rebrand mid-history; sub-team work in different registers; experimental campaigns alongside flagship campaigns). Stage 5 produces content with internal divergence. The operator identifies which register is current and operational; treats older or experimental registers as continuity references, not authority. Per the sparse-articulation note's condition 4 plus PR #241's Condition 5 (divergent).
- **Past campaigns more operationally credible than current documentation.** The articulated input categories (brand platform; VIS; photography style guide; application guidelines) are sparse, outdated, or contradictory; the past-campaigns archive is rich and coherent. Per visual-as-fidelity-strategy, demonstrated work carries when articulated guidance diverges or is absent. Stage 5 becomes the architecture's primary source of brand-system content in this case, with Stages 1–4 reduced to thin overlays. The operator may use Stage 5 evidence to backfill what Stages 1–4 should articulate (an intake-driven update to current articulation; operator-side, not architectural).

### Defers when sparse or absent

When past-campaigns content is sparse or absent (the brand has thin campaign history; archive is incomplete or partially lost), Stage 5 falls through to:

- **Stage 4 application guidelines** as the closest cross-validation surface for mode-activation and touchpoint-set (less load-bearing without past-campaign validation).
- **Stage 1 asset library** as the closest cross-validation surface for executed conventions (asset library provides imagery without the campaign envelope, which is less informative for arc continuity and channel propagation but adequate for execution-level conventions).
- **Stage 6 worked-example defaults** for content that cannot be cross-validated against any available evidence.

### Iteration cases

Stage 5 surfaces evolution that contradicts earlier stages (sparse-articulation condition 4's past-vs-current case): newer campaigns showing the brand moving in a direction older articulation does not name. The operator returns to whichever earlier stage holds the contradicted content and resolves per the layered fallback chain — typically current guidelines carry as default, but if past-campaign deviation is the intended evolution, the operator re-articulates the upstream guideline. The feedback loop from Stage 5 back to Stages 2–4 is part of the iterative intake pattern PR #241 named.

## Four-Mode Pressure

Past-campaigns content loads asymmetrically across the four worked-example workflow modes. Each mode draws on different past-campaigns content, and the evidence depth supporting each mode's intake is asymmetric. The pressure below is conceptual; full-flow operational pressure on past-campaigns-shaped intake remains untested across the modes.

### SKU-driven mode

**What past campaigns supply.** Lightest contribution of any mode. Past campaigns provide historical product-photography reference precedents where past campaigns included per-product imagery; cross-campaign continuity evidence for product-photography conventions; historical packet-template shapes for SKU-driven moments where they appeared inside campaign envelopes. Where the brand runs SKU-driven product imagery outside campaign contexts entirely (a steady-state catalog program), past campaigns may supply nothing beyond historical convention precedent.

**Operational evidence.** PKT-SKU-007 / 009 / 010 full-flow work was operational at SKU mode without explicit past-campaigns validation surface. SKU mode's primary fidelity strategy is per-SKU `product_image` plus packet-level reference (REF-002 in the Phase 1 evidence — itself a past-campaign-style continuity reference reused across packets). Past-campaigns intake for SKU mode adds historical-pattern context without changing the mode's operational shape.

### Collection / merchandising mode

**What past campaigns supply.** Moderate contribution. Past campaigns supply historical grouping patterns where past campaigns featured collection-shaped moments (a campaign anchored around a curated edit; a campaign featuring a capsule release); cross-axis selection criteria precedent (which grouping logics have proven workable); historical family-coherence patterns at collection scope; cross-channel propagation of collection content (how a collection moved from lookbook to email to social).

**Operational evidence.** PKT-COLL-001 full-flow operational evidence covered one collection-mode packet without explicit past-campaigns validation. The collection-mode operational deflation of the composite-anchor candidate is mode-internal; past-campaigns intake adds historical-pattern context that would inform similar future packets but does not change the deflated candidate's status.

### Marketing / message-driven mode

**What past campaigns supply.** Moderate-to-heavy contribution. Past campaigns supply historical message / offer / communication content from promotional campaigns (where preserved); channel-adaptation patterns across email, social, banner, in-store promotional, OOH; cross-touchpoint message-variant patterns; historical attention-budget treatment under message pressure. Promotional past campaigns are often better-preserved than editorial campaigns at the message-content layer (commercial-objective campaigns frequently preserve message articulation alongside execution); editorial campaigns may carry less explicit message content.

**Operational evidence.** PKT-MSG-001 cycle 1 work is structural-proof depth only. Past-campaigns intake for marketing mode produces content that absorbs into existing prose carriers; full-flow operational pressure on marketing mode (which would exercise past-campaigns-derived message and channel-propagation content) is untested.

### Brand campaign / editorial mode

**What past campaigns supply.** The heaviest contribution of any mode and the most load-bearing past-campaigns content overall. Past campaigns supply: cross-campaign continuity for campaign-world references (per PR #239's fourth reference function); editorial-arc patterns; voice continuity demonstration across editorial work; review-context conventions exercised across review boards; family-coherence patterns at campaign scope; per-channel campaign propagation; reuse / derivative patterns from past-campaign-anchor assets. Past campaigns ARE the mode's primary evidence base.

**Operational evidence.** PKT-CAMP-004 bounded full-flow at the kitchen-prep packet operationalized one mode-specific packet within the Warm Restraint campaign-world envelope. PKT-CAMP-001 cycle 1 rerun was structural-proof depth. The Campaign-Driven D2C Home-Goods v1 base setup carried the 95-field schema mirror but did not exercise full-flow at multiple-campaign depth. Cross-campaign continuity pressure (the load-bearing past-campaigns-intake question for campaign mode) is unearned operationally — the project has worked within single-campaign envelopes rather than across multi-campaign brand history.

### Mode-asymmetry summary

The pressure clarifies what past-campaigns stage-load looks like across modes: heaviest in campaign / editorial mode (past campaigns ARE the primary evidence base; cross-campaign continuity is mode-load-bearing); moderate-to-heavy in marketing mode (channel-adaptation history and message-archive evidence); moderate in collection mode (grouping-pattern validation); lightest in SKU-driven mode (historical product-photography precedent only). None of the four modes has been operationally pressured on past-campaigns-shaped intake at cross-campaign depth.

## Trace / Scale-Legibility Requirements

PR #239 named brand digestion as the first of nine creative-discretion sites; past-campaigns intake operates across the same site plus the *reference selection* site (the second per PR #239's table) where past-campaign assets are selected as upstream references for current work, and the *override / exception handling* site (the ninth) where past-campaign deviation from current articulation must be resolved. Past-campaigns content sits at the intersection of these three sites.

Per PR #239's scale-legibility test criterion: a non-author operator should be able to reconstruct why a given past-campaign asset, pattern, or precedent applies at the layer it does, from the architecture alone. Past campaigns as an intake site stress this criterion in distinctive ways because the temporal dimension introduces an additional axis of legibility — a non-author operator must reconstruct not only the layer assignment but also the temporal-state assignment.

### What a non-author operator would need to reconstruct

- **Which campaign evidence was inspected.** The campaign archive's coverage may exceed what intake actually examined; downstream readers cannot distinguish "this campaign was reviewed and contributed content" from "this campaign exists in the archive but was not reviewed." Currently absent; overlaps with PR #241's presence-of-inspection trace dimension.
- **Which campaign is current vs historical vs superseded.** The temporal-state assignment for each past-campaign content piece — does it represent current operating reality, historical reference, or actively-superseded posture? This is the past-campaigns-specific trace dimension; it does not cleanly map to PR #241's five named dimensions and surfaces here as a candidate **temporal-state trace** (sixth candidate dimension). Held; not proposed as carrier.
- **Which content was lifted from campaign evidence.** Lifted-verbatim content (a specific past-campaign asset linked as current reference; a verbatim phrase from a campaign brief carried into current intent text). Overlaps with PR #241's derivation-basis trace dimension; specifies the "lifted" sub-value.
- **Which content was inferred from campaign patterns.** Inferred-from-cross-campaign-pattern content (a recurring narrative motif inferred from three campaigns; a photographic posture inferred from sustained execution). Overlaps with derivation-basis under the "inferred-from-evidence" sub-value; the evidence-basis is specifically cross-campaign pattern recognition.
- **Which content was rejected as obsolete or non-authoritative.** Rejected-because-superseded content (past-campaign content the operator examined but excluded from current intake because it reflects superseded brand posture). Currently absent; overlaps with negative-evidence handling — at the intake site rather than at the asset-library negative-evidence handling the asset-library deepening surfaced.
- **How conflicts between past campaigns and current guidelines were resolved.** Conflict-resolution trace for the case PR #241's Condition 3 (contradictory) handles at the cross-source-divergence level — specifically the past-campaigns-vs-current-articulation conflict and its resolution. Overlaps with PR #241's fifth trace dimension (contradiction-resolution); applies specifically to the temporal-conflict case.

### What is currently prose-carried

Past-campaigns content distributes downstream into existing prose carriers when integrated into current intake: `creative_intent` text (historical-pattern-informed authorship); `bounded_creative_discretion` text (where historical execution bounds the discretion zone); `capture_reason` / `capture_notes` text (curation reasoning referencing past-campaign precedent); reference linkage in `approved_reference_keys` for past-campaign assets elevated to current reference status. The prose carriers absorb the content; the source-attribution, temporal-state assignment, and conflict-resolution trace do not surface structurally.

### What remains operator-continuous

The full chain from past-campaigns archive inspection through temporal-state classification (current / historical / superseded), content lift / inference / rejection decisions, and conflict resolution against current articulation lives in operator continuity. At one-operator continuous-context scale, the operator carries the chain verbally; at any scale beyond one operator, the chain is structurally absent. The temporal-state trace dimension makes the scale-legibility frontier sharper for past-campaigns intake than for the other input categories — the same content can be lifted as current authority, retained as historical reference, or rejected as superseded, and the architecture has no carrier that distinguishes these three downstream uses.

### No carrier proposal

This artifact does not propose minimum-trace fields, structural carriers for past-campaigns content, or sub-classification of derivation-basis to accommodate the temporal-state dimension. It names the trace requirements at the current scale-legibility frontier and stops there. Per PR #241's posture, scale beyond operator continuity is treated as an architectural premise of the project (per grounding-note v58 + [`docs/architecture.md`](architecture.md)'s Scale-of-Operation reframe); the trace requirements name where legibility currently relies on operator continuity, not what would close the gap structurally. The temporal-state trace candidate joins the inventory of held trace-related candidates without being proposed for adjudication here.

## Earned vs Held

What this deepening clarifies at this evidence depth:

- **Past campaigns are historical execution evidence, not brand doctrine by default.** The load-bearing analytical distinction. Past campaigns may validate articulated brand-system content, contradict it, supersede it, or pressure it, but they do not themselves constitute authority over what the brand should do next — doctrine sits in the articulation-based input categories (brand platform; VIS; photography style guide; application guidelines). Where current articulation is sparse or absent and past campaigns demonstrate sustained pattern, past campaigns carry conditional authority — but the relationship is one of demonstrated history with conditional weight, not articulated direction.
- **Past campaigns are the campaign-as-execution-unit input category, distinct from asset library at the imagery layer.** Asset library is approved imagery as a flat or thinly-organized population; past campaigns are execution-units of which imagery is one element among campaign-world, arc, channel-propagation, governance, and outcomes. The categories overlap at the imagery layer; past campaigns carry the execution-unit envelope asset library does not.
- **Past campaigns introduce a temporal dimension the other five input categories do not carry structurally.** Current / historical / superseded temporal-state classification per content piece is past-campaigns-specific and stresses scale-legibility at a dimension PR #241's five trace dimensions do not cleanly cover. A candidate sixth trace dimension — **temporal-state trace** — surfaces here without being proposed for carrier promotion. It joins the inventory of held trace-related candidates.
- **Past campaigns are the only input category that contributes structurally-recognizable content at the governed-asset layer.** Via the governed-output continuity reference function (per PR #239's ninth reference function), past-campaign governed assets become upstream inputs for derivative packets. The reuse / derivative governance question — under what conditions, with what governance applied — is the held question this contribution sharpens without resolving.
- **Past-campaigns content asymmetrically loads the four modes.** Heaviest in campaign / editorial mode (past campaigns ARE the primary evidence base); moderate-to-heavy in marketing mode; moderate in collection mode; lightest in SKU-driven mode. The asymmetry mirrors past-campaigns content's distinctive contribution at the mode-specific layer plus the campaign-mode-specific load at the brand-system layer (via cross-campaign continuity evidence).
- **All six brand-system input categories are now at Phase 2 deepening depth.** With this artifact, the six-category set (photography style guide; asset library; brand platform; VIS; application guidelines; past campaigns) is complete at deepening depth. Whether the architecture's next move is a six-category synthesis, an operational pressure test against any input category, a structured-form absorption, or a different direction is a strategic decision out of scope for this deepening.
