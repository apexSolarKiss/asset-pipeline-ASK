# Brand-Intake Artifact Contract // Boundary Cases (v1)

## Posture

```text
companion to brand-intake-artifact-contract-v1.md
synthetic cases with expected placements, written before the run that tests them
fictive brands only · invented content · no real-brand material
not a validator · not a schema · not a scoring standard beyond these cases
self-superseding with the contract
```

## How to run

1. Give a reader the contract, [`brand-intake-artifact-contract-v1.md`](brand-intake-artifact-contract-v1.md), and the **Statement** column only. The reader does not see this file's expectations.
2. The reader places each statement, or each limb of it, in one of the contract's destinations:
   - brand image system;
   - image-production profile;
   - production brief;
   - execution plan;
   - evidence-and-authority record;
   - review presentation;
   - other Axis-B scope (category / product-class);
   - retired.

   Where the case asks, the reader also states the authority effect.
3. Compare the reader's placements with the expected ones. A case passes when every required limb lands where expected, nothing lands outside the expected or allowed destinations, and any stated authority effect matches.
4. **A failing case is evidence against the contract's clarity, or against the case, not against the reader.** Record it, and correct the contract or the case. Do not quietly re-run until it passes.

The brands are fictive:
- **Example Furniture Brand:** the brand of [`examples/sku-furniture-brand-style-spec-example.md`](examples/sku-furniture-brand-style-spec-example.md).
- **Example Homes:** a townhouse builder whose images start from photographs of unfinished interiors.

## What these cases test

- **Visual grammar versus delivery requirements:** cases 1–4, 15, 18–21 and 24 (a set-level relationship stated with a number, against case 18's allocation).
- **Instance facts versus recurring design vocabulary:** cases 5–7 and 23.
- **Review notes kept out of content:** cases 8–9.
- **Authorization unchanged after faithful relocation or rewording:** cases 10 and 28.
- **No authority gained through clean rendering:** case 11.
- **Format changes without image-system changes:** cases 3 and 15.
- **Trial-scoped direction without automatic promotion:** cases 12–13.
- **Recurrence diagnosed, not promoted:** case 25.
- **Use within scope versus a change of scope:** cases 26 and 27.
- **A depiction target drawn from several sources, with no redundant authorization:** case 17.
- **Also covered:** execution, permission and purpose (cases 14, 16 and 22).

## Negative control

The omnibus pattern places everything in "the brand guide", which is the image system, and states no authority effect. Applied to these cases, it passes only cases 1, 4, 5, 19, 21 and 24. It fails every other case, including case 7 (its brief limb), case 11 (its authority effect) and case 28 (its authority effect). A grader that does not fail the omnibus answers is not testing the contract.

## Checking a composed production ask

For a composition check using a worked artifact set, fix the judging criteria from the governing source of intent and authorized rulings before the run. Do not derive the standard solely from the artifacts being tested: an artifact error must not become a passing expectation.

Name the composer's permitted inputs separately from the judge's governing sources, and record the identities of both. Judge preservation of the production scope, applicable authority, unresolved requirements and permitted variation against those sources. Distinguish defects in the input artifacts or their authority record from unsupported additions by the composer.

Score only the checks actually exercised. Preserve failures and disclose grader corrections or post-test edits; neither a corrected artifact nor a corrected record is evidence of a successful retest.
