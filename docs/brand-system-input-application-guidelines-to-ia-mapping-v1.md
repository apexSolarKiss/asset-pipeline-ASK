## What Application Guidelines Are

Application guidelines are the brand's articulation of *how it applies itself* — the rules for moving brand-system content from definition into operation across the surfaces where the brand actually appears. Distinct from each of the other five input categories along clear axes.

### Distinction from the other five input categories

- **Brand platform** articulates *what* the brand is (values, voice, stance, audience, promise, authority, narrative themes, exclusion boundaries) — propositional identity. Application guidelines articulate *how* that identity moves into operational use across surfaces.
- **Visual identity system** articulates the brand's formal visual constants (logo, typography, color codes, grid systems) plus their application guides — the rules for applying those *constants*. Application guidelines, as a broader input category, cover the application logic for *all* brand-system content (constants, conventions, voice register, photography posture), not just the formal visual constants. The VIS deepening's seventh component (application guides) is nested inside this broader category; the boundary is scope, not exclusion.
- **Photography / image style guide** articulates conventions for how the brand *photographs its subject* — lighting, composition, framing, treatment. Application guidelines articulate how the resulting imagery (and the brand system around it) *gets used* across touchpoints — what aspect ratios, what copy-overlay tolerance, what attention budgets, what per-channel adaptations.
- **Asset library** demonstrates the brand's conventions in operation through accumulated approved work. Application guidelines articulate the *rules* the operation follows; the asset library shows the *outcomes* those rules produced.
- **Past campaigns** demonstrate the brand operating across time — campaign-shaped execution with arc continuity, voice continuity, family-coherence patterns. Application guidelines describe how the brand intends to operate; past campaigns demonstrate how it has operated. Application guidelines are forward-looking and prescriptive; past campaigns are backward-looking and demonstrative.

The boundary running across all five distinctions: application guidelines are the *operationalization layer* of the brand system. The other categories define what the brand is (platform), how it formally appears (VIS), how it visually executes (photography style guide; asset library), or how it has run (past campaigns). Application guidelines define how all of that becomes operational use.

### What counts as application guidelines input

Application guidelines content typically articulates the brand's application rules across seven to nine components. Each is conceptually distinct even when a brand collapses several into one document or none into structured form at all.

#### Per-channel application rules

How the brand applies across distinct channels — web, email, social, print, retail, packaging, environment, OOH (out-of-home), motion / video, native applications. Per-channel rules typically articulate: tone register adjustments per channel; visual treatment adaptations; brand-identifier presence rules; CTA conventions; brand-element prominence; channel-specific exclusions. A brand with mature application guidelines articulates per-channel rules at considerable depth; a brand with thin application guidelines may articulate only one or two channels and leave others to operator practice.

#### Per-format adaptations

How the brand adapts to format specifications — aspect ratios (portrait / landscape / square / vertical / specific platform ratios); resolution and color-mode requirements; layout grids per surface; safe-zone and bleed conventions. Per-format adaptations bridge the brand's formal visual identity (VIS) to the operational requirements each surface imposes. Format specifications are typically the most pre-structured content of application guidelines because format requirements come from external technical constraints (platform specs; print specs).

#### Per-touchpoint constraints

Touchpoint-specific operational constraints: attention budgets (a billboard has seconds of viewing attention; a print catalog has minutes); copy-overlay tolerance (how much text can sit on an image at this touchpoint without breaking visual integrity); message-density rules; brand-identifier-placement rules (where the logo sits; how prominently); call-to-action conventions per touchpoint. Per-touchpoint constraints distinguish *channel* (a medium-level category like "social") from *touchpoint* (a specific surface like "Instagram in-feed post" vs "Instagram story") — the same channel often spans multiple touchpoints with different constraints.

#### Per-context boundaries

Context-specific application rules: usage-context restrictions (which assets may appear in editorial context vs paid placement vs internal use); in-frame vs out-of-frame requirements (subject placement; safe zones for headlines); brand-hierarchy rules when the brand appears alongside other brands (co-marketing; sponsored placement; third-party context); regulatory or category-specific restrictions (medical / legal / financial disclaimers; age-gated content rules). Context boundaries often emerge from compliance or partnership requirements rather than aesthetic preference.

#### Cross-channel coherence rules

Rules governing how the brand maintains coherence across channels — when the same campaign runs across web, email, social, and print, what stays constant and what adapts. Cross-channel coherence rules typically articulate: which visual anchors must persist across channel adaptations; what voice continuity is required; how mode-specific content (a marketing message; a campaign hero) propagates across the channel set; what kind of channel-specific variation is allowed within shared brand identity.

#### Mode-activation signals

The brand's articulated assumptions about which workflow modes (per Phase 1's worked-example mode framing — SKU-driven product imagery; collection / merchandising; marketing / message-driven; brand campaign / editorial) the brand operates in. Mode-activation may be explicit ("we operate four campaigns per year; we maintain ongoing catalog photography; we do quarterly collection releases") or implicit (touchpoint-list inventory implies which modes are active). The same application guidelines document often signals all four modes asymmetrically — a retail brand's application guidelines may be heavy on SKU-driven and collection modes, lighter on campaign.

#### Role and slot implications

Per-touchpoint role inventory: which slot roles (HERO / PROFILE / DETAIL for SKU mode; HERO_GROUP / DETAIL_ADJACENCY for collection mode; hero / tight / supporting for marketing mode; per-arc roles for campaign mode) the brand typically uses per touchpoint, and what touchpoint-specific role conventions apply (e.g., "social hero is square-cropped; web hero is wide-cropped").

#### Approval / governance implications

Per-touchpoint approval rules where the brand articulates them: which touchpoints require senior approval before launch; which require legal review; which require brand-team approval vs operator latitude; which override conventions apply per-touchpoint. Governance content here overlaps brand platform's authority / decision posture but is touchpoint-specific rather than brand-wide.

#### Production-context boundaries

Rules for production contexts the brand operates in: in-house production vs agency production vs freelance vs AI-assisted; what each context is authorized to produce without additional review; how production-context choice affects approval and governance. Production-context boundaries are increasingly relevant as production methods diversify; many brands have not yet articulated them at depth.

The seven-to-nine components overlap in practice. A brand's per-channel rules often embed per-format adaptations and per-touchpoint constraints; cross-channel coherence rules typically reference both channel and format content; mode-activation signals are often implicit in the touchpoint inventory rather than explicitly named. Intake addresses each component for what it carries; cross-component overlap surfaces additively rather than as conflict requiring resolution.

## What Application Guidelines Contribute

The IA receives application-guidelines content as: per-touchpoint operational constraints; format-level rules; brand-identifier presence patterns; mode-activation signals; per-touchpoint role and slot conventions; cross-channel coherence requirements; channel-specific exclusions and boundaries; and approval / governance implications where the brand articulates them per-touchpoint. The contribution shapes intake's understanding of *where* the brand applies its system, *how* it does so, and *with what constraints*.

Application guidelines provide content the other input categories do not: brand platform supplies authority posture but not per-touchpoint application; VIS supplies brand-wide constants but not per-channel coherence rules at scale; photography style guide supplies conventions but not the operational constraints those conventions must satisfy per touchpoint; asset library demonstrates outcomes but not the rules behind them; past campaigns demonstrate campaign-shaped execution but not the steady-state application logic. The application-guidelines contribution is the rule-shaped operationalization content that sits between brand-system definition and per-packet production.

Two limits on the contribution:

- **Application guidelines do not author packet-level content.** They constrain it. A packet's `creative_intent` text comes from brand platform + photography style guide content; application guidelines tell intake what touchpoint-specific constraints that creative intent must operate within. The packet's content authorship draws on multiple input categories; application guidelines is the constraint layer, not the authorship layer.
- **Application guidelines do not resolve subject-specific or campaign-specific conventions.** They cover steady-state application; past campaigns cover campaign-specific instantiations of the brand system; photography style guide covers subject-specific photographic conventions. Where a specific campaign or subject requires deviation from steady-state application rules, that deviation is operator-mediated or campaign-articulated, not application-guidelines-articulated.

## IA Layer Mapping

Application guidelines content distributes across IA layers in a distinctive pattern: lightest at the most-upstream layer (brand-system) and the most-downstream layers (candidate generation; governed asset); concentrated at the mode-specific, packet, slot, and curation layers where touchpoint constraints become operational.

### Brand-system layer (currently unmodeled in IA)

**Receives.** A small share of application-guidelines content: cross-channel coherence principles operating at brand-wide scope ("the brand maintains consistent voice across all surfaces"; "brand-identifier always appears in defined zones"). These are application principles that hold above any specific mode or touchpoint; they belong conceptually at the brand-system layer.

**Resolution shape.** The brand-system layer has no first-class carrier (per [`docs/architecture.md`](architecture.md)'s Brand-System Layer Status named decision; three live shapes; hybrid most likely). Application-guidelines content at this layer currently flows downstream as operator-interpretive guidance into prose carriers, the same way brand platform abstractions and photography style guide brand-level conventions do. The cross-channel coherence principles are content for the unmodeled-but-named layer; intake does not resolve the structural decision.

### Category / product-class layer (currently signaled-only in IA)

**Receives.** Category-specific application content where brands articulate it: per-category touchpoint variation ("our furniture line uses lifestyle context in web; our apparel line uses flat-lay in web"); per-product-class application adaptations. Most brands articulate application guidelines at brand-wide rather than per-category depth; category-specific application content is often implicit in the asset library's per-category organization rather than in application-guidelines articulation.

**Resolution shape.** Category presence is signaled-only in current IA; carried by worked-example naming and per-SKU descriptive fields. Category-specific application content (when articulated) flows into per-category operator practice; first-class category-aesthetic carriers remain unearned per Apex Named Limitation #1.

### Mode-specific layer (varies per mode)

**Receives.** The strongest application-guidelines contribution at this layer. Application guidelines supply: mode activation (which modes the brand operates in); per-mode touchpoint inventory (which touchpoints belong to SKU mode vs marketing mode vs campaign mode); mode-specific application conventions (collection-grouping touchpoint rules; marketing-message channel rules; campaign-arc per-channel propagation rules).

**Resolution shape.** Each non-SKU mode carries at least one mode-specific layer between category and packet (collection / grouping; message / offer / communication; campaign concept + review-context / campaign-world coherence). Application-guidelines content here populates the mode-specific layer's operational content — what the touchpoints look like, what constraints apply per touchpoint within the mode. Carriers vary per mode (some prose; some signaled via mode-specific fields; some paper-pressed as held composite-anchor candidates like `messages` / `briefs` for marketing and `campaign_concept` for campaign — none earned).

### Packet layer (the strongest current carrier)

**Receives.** Per-touchpoint constraint content that the packet must operate within: aspect ratios; copy-overlay tolerance; attention-budget assumptions; brand-identifier presence rules; per-touchpoint role inventory. Packet-level recurring shapes the brand uses per touchpoint ("we always need a hero plus three companions per email" — application-guidelines-driven; the packet template the brand articulates).

**Resolution shape.** Packet layer carries structurally explicit fields (`business_intent`, `creative_intent`, `bounded_creative_discretion`, `constraint_rules`, `approved_reference_keys`, products relation). Application-guidelines content flows into `constraint_rules` (touchpoint-specific operational rules), `bounded_creative_discretion` (where touchpoint constraints bound the discretion zone), `business_intent` (what touchpoint the packet serves), and `approved_reference_keys` (per-touchpoint reference imagery from the asset library). The packet's over-load on aesthetic + intent + (mode-specific) overlay responsibility extends to a touchpoint-constraint overlay when the brand operates across touchpoints with materially different constraints.

### Slot layer (the role-specific narrowing carrier)

**Receives.** Per-slot touchpoint constraints: aspect ratios per role-and-touchpoint (HERO in social vs HERO in print); per-role copy-overlay tolerance; per-role attention-budget assumptions; per-role brand-identifier rules; per-role per-touchpoint composition adaptations.

**Resolution shape.** `slot_role` enum carries role identity; `slot_prompt` text carries role-specific composition instructions including touchpoint-shaped composition rules; packet-level reference imagery surfaces into slot inputs via lookup. The aspect-ratio-as-attribute candidate (paper-pressed in marketing- and campaign-mode sketches per Phase 1; named held in [`docs/structured-ia-model-v1.md`](structured-ia-model-v1.md)'s held-candidates list) would directly support the per-touchpoint per-slot aspect-ratio content application guidelines surface; it is held, not earned.

### Candidate generation layer

**Receives.** Generally nothing directly. Candidate generation is transient by design and produces no first-class entity; application-guidelines constraints surface at the slot layer (via `slot_prompt`) and at the curation seam (via curation criteria).

**Resolution shape.** Application-guidelines content does not flow into the candidate-generation layer; it bounds the layer's operation upstream (via slot_prompt content) and downstream (via curation criteria).

### Curation seam

**Receives.** Per-touchpoint judgment criteria: what counts as touchpoint-appropriate; whether a candidate satisfies the touchpoint's operational constraints; brand-identifier presence verification; copy-overlay tolerance verification; aspect-ratio-and-composition fit for the touchpoint. Plus: approval / governance content where the brand articulates per-touchpoint approval rules (which touchpoints require senior approval; which apply legal review; which apply brand-team approval).

**Resolution shape.** The curation seam carries clean structural carriers (five-axis provenance + `curator` + `curation_pattern` for candidate selection; `governed_output_status` + `governed_output_flag` + `asset_attachment` + `slot_status` lifecycle for governance-bearing curation). Application-guidelines content flows into curation criteria as the *content* of judgment — `capture_reason` / `capture_notes` text describing why a candidate satisfies (or fails) the touchpoint's operational constraints. Per-touchpoint approval rules feed governance posture (which `curation_pattern` values apply per touchpoint; what governance authority chain applies). Approval rules as structured per-touchpoint metadata is not currently an IA carrier; held content lives in operator practice.

### Governed asset layer

**Receives.** Reuse / derivative governance content where the brand articulates it per-touchpoint (e.g., "campaign-mode hero shots may be reused as catalog product-detail references; marketing-mode message imagery may not be lifted as evergreen product imagery without re-approval"). Per-touchpoint usage-rights and channel-scope content where the brand articulates them (usage rights for a specific touchpoint context; cross-touchpoint reuse rules).

**Resolution shape.** Governed-output continuity reference (per PR #239's ninth reference function) is currently unmodeled at first-class depth; reuse / derivative rules are named in the apex artifact as adjacent not-yet-hardenable concerns. Application-guidelines content here is prose-carried in operator practice; the architecture absorbs reuse decisions case-by-case without structural enforcement.

## Four-Mode Pressure

Application guidelines distribute differently across the four worked-example workflow modes. Each mode draws on different application-guidelines content, and the evidence depth supporting each mode's intake is asymmetric. The pressure below is conceptual; full-flow operational pressure on application-guidelines-shaped intake remains untested across the modes.

### SKU-driven mode

**What application guidelines supply.** Per-product-photography-touchpoint constraints (catalog vs e-commerce-listing vs PDP-supporting imagery); per-touchpoint role inventory (HERO / PROFILE / DETAIL aspect-ratio rules per touchpoint); per-product-touchpoint aspect-ratio and resolution requirements; per-touchpoint brand-identifier presence rules (if any apply to per-product photography). For SKU-driven mode, the application-guidelines content is often lighter than other modes because product photography touchpoints tend to share more constraints across surfaces than marketing or campaign touchpoints do.

**Operational evidence.** PKT-SKU-007 / 009 / 010 full-flow work was operational at a single primary touchpoint (the product photography touchpoint that surfaces into catalog / PDP / e-commerce listing). Cross-touchpoint pressure on the same SKU's imagery has not been tested. Application-guidelines intake for SKU-driven mode is therefore at structural-claim depth, not operational-deflation depth.

### Collection / merchandising mode

**What application guidelines supply.** Per-collection-touchpoint constraints (lookbook vs capsule-edit page vs catalog spread); cross-slot consistency requirements per touchpoint (a lookbook spread has different consistency requirements than a capsule edit page); per-touchpoint role inventory variants (HERO_GROUP vs DETAIL_ADJACENCY at different touchpoints).

**Operational evidence.** PKT-COLL-001 full-flow operational evidence covered one collection-mode packet at one touchpoint context. Cross-touchpoint application-guidelines pressure on collection-mode imagery is untested. The 1:N slot-product cardinality strain that the collection-mode operational test deflated is touchpoint-independent; application-guidelines content for collection mode adds touchpoint-specific constraints on top of the slot-product cardinality pattern.

### Marketing / message-driven mode

**What application guidelines supply.** The heaviest application-guidelines load of any mode. Marketing mode is touchpoint-shaped at every slot — email vs social vs banner vs in-store promotional vs OOH each impose distinct aspect-ratio, attention-budget, copy-overlay, brand-identifier, and CTA conventions. Per-touchpoint application content is the dominant content of marketing-mode intake.

**Operational evidence.** PKT-MSG-001 cycle 1 work is structural-proof depth only; full-flow operational pressure on marketing-mode is untested. Application-guidelines content for marketing mode is paper-pressed in Phase 1's marketing-mode sketch (`messages` / `briefs` first-class candidate; structured slot-message-element fields; `selection_axis` enum; aspect-ratio-as-attribute) and remains operationally untested. Application-guidelines intake for marketing mode therefore produces content that absorbs into existing prose carriers; what would change under operational pressure is unprobed.

### Brand campaign / editorial mode

**What application guidelines supply.** Per-channel campaign-arc propagation rules (how a campaign moves from anchor surface — typically print or premium digital — through derivative surfaces with appropriate adaptation); per-touchpoint campaign-world coherence requirements (which campaign visuals must persist across channels; what touchpoint-specific adaptations preserve campaign-world integrity); per-touchpoint review-context conventions (which review boards convene per touchpoint; what touchpoint-specific approval chains apply). Application-guidelines content for campaign mode is moderate-to-heavy depending on the brand's campaign-channel reach.

**Operational evidence.** PKT-CAMP-004 bounded full-flow at the kitchen-prep packet covered the campaign-mode fork-resolver test at one packet's depth without cross-touchpoint pressure; PKT-CAMP-001 cycle 1 rerun was structural-proof depth. Application-guidelines content for campaign mode interacts with PR #239's curation semantic split: per-touchpoint approval chains feed governance-bearing curation criteria, while per-channel propagation rules can shape upstream authorship-bearing discretion at prompt-authoring and prompt-remediation (the seventh and eighth discretion sites per PR #239). Operational pressure on this interaction is unearned.

### Mode-asymmetry summary

The pressure clarifies what application-guidelines stage-load looks like across modes without proposing per-mode intake mechanics: heaviest in marketing mode (touchpoint-shaped at every slot); moderate-to-heavy in campaign mode (per-channel propagation + per-touchpoint review-context); moderate in collection mode (cross-slot consistency per touchpoint); lightest in SKU-driven mode (shared constraints across product-photography touchpoints). None of the four modes has been operationally pressured on application-guidelines-shaped content at cross-touchpoint depth.

## Relationship to PR #241 Extraction Sequence

PR #241 named application as Stage 4 of the seven-stage extraction sequence (`evidence → articulated conventions → abstraction → application → validation → defaults → inference/authorship`). Application guidelines as an input category primarily occupies that stage. The stage-relationships in PR #241's framing apply here:

### Stage 4 primary occupation

Application-guidelines content is what Stage 4 // Application produces: per-touchpoint and per-mode application content; which workflow modes the brand operates in; per-mode templates and recurring patterns; mode-specific overlay content (collection-grouping logic; marketing-message conventions; campaign-concept articulation); per-touchpoint role inventory and aspect-ratio / attention-budget / copy-overlay rules.

### Dependencies on earlier stages

- **Stage 1 (evidence).** Asset library segments organized by touchpoint validate the brand's stated touchpoint set. Where application guidelines name a touchpoint the asset library does not cover, the gap is real — either the brand has not yet executed at that touchpoint, or the asset library is incomplete relative to the application set. Stage 4 must reconcile stated touchpoint set against demonstrated touchpoint coverage; the visual-as-fidelity-strategy claim from the apex aesthetic-layering pass governs the reconciliation (where stated and demonstrated diverge, demonstrated typically carries — but for application set specifically, the divergence may reflect future-intended use rather than current operating reality, which complicates the default).
- **Stage 2 (articulated conventions).** Photography style guide channel adaptations (the photography deepening's tenth dimension) overlap application guidelines per-channel and per-format content. The two need reconciliation when they articulate different rules for the same touchpoint. When photography style guide channel adaptations and application-guidelines per-channel rules conflict, the operator resolves per intake context — typically application guidelines carries at the touchpoint-constraint layer (aspect ratios; copy-overlay tolerance), while photography style guide carries at the photographic-treatment layer (lighting; composition) even within the touchpoint.
- **Stage 3 (abstraction).** Brand-platform authority / decision posture binds application choices — which touchpoints require which approval; where curator latitude exists; what override authority applies per-touchpoint. Application-guidelines per-touchpoint approval rules typically inherit from brand-platform authority posture at brand-wide scope and refine it per-touchpoint.

### Validation dependency on later stages

- **Stage 5 (validation via past campaigns).** Past-campaigns evidence validates Stage 4's stated mode activation. A brand whose application guidelines name campaign mode but whose past campaigns are absent (pre-launch; new-launch; lapsed-campaign-program brand) has stated mode activation that historical evidence does not support; the operator notes the divergence as forward-looking-intent rather than operating-reality.

### Defers when sparse or absent

When application guidelines content is sparse or absent (the brand has not articulated per-touchpoint application rules), Stage 4 falls through to:

- **Past-campaigns evidence** (Stage 5) — the brand's accumulated execution implies its application practice even where rules are not articulated. Cross-campaign touchpoint inventory surfaces the brand's actual active mode set.
- **Asset-library segmentation** (Stage 1) — some libraries segment by touchpoint (campaign / catalog / social / email folders), exposing the implicit application set the brand operates against. Library organization is itself application-guidelines-shaped content.
- **Phase 1 worked-example defaults** (Stage 6) — when all of Stages 1, 2, 4, and 5 produce thin content, the worked-example defaults (HERO / PROFILE / DETAIL for SKU mode; HERO_GROUP / DETAIL_ADJACENCY for collection mode; per-mode role taxonomies) provide brand-agnostic baseline application content.

### Iteration cases

Application-guidelines intake is iterative when later-stage pressure surfaces. Stage 5 past-campaigns evidence may show the brand operating in a mode application guidelines do not name (the operator returns to Stage 4 to either expand the stated mode set or mark past-campaign work as historical-not-continued). Stage 7 inference may surface that the brand's actual application practice exceeds its articulated guidelines (operator returns to Stage 4 to note the implicit application content). Stages 1–3 may surface content that pressures application-guidelines rules (cross-channel coherence rules diverge from observed library segmentation; brand-platform authority posture conflicts with stated per-touchpoint approval rules).

## Trace / Scale-Legibility Requirements

PR #239 named brand digestion as the first of nine creative-discretion sites; application-guidelines intake operates across the same site plus the *layer translation* site (the third per PR #239's table) where mode-specific overlay content distributes from brand-system + category-level content into packet and slot layers. Application-guidelines content sits at the intersection: it's both intake (resolving what the brand says about its application) and layer translation (deciding how application content shapes per-mode and per-touchpoint downstream content).

Per PR #239's scale-legibility test criterion: a non-author operator should be able to reconstruct why a given touchpoint constraint, brand-identifier rule, or mode-activation decision applies at the layer it does, from the architecture alone. Application guidelines as an intake site stresses this criterion at multiple layers:

### What a non-author operator would need to reconstruct

- **Mode activation.** Which workflow modes the brand operates in, and the basis for that determination (articulated application guidelines / past-campaigns evidence / asset-library segmentation / operator inference). Operator-independent legibility currently absent; mode activation surfaces only through implicit signal (the existence of mode-specific packets in the IA implies the mode is active, but the *reasoning* for activation lives in operator practice).
- **Per-touchpoint constraint origin.** Where a slot's aspect-ratio constraint, copy-overlay tolerance, or attention-budget assumption came from (which application-guidelines source; which touchpoint articulation; whether stated or operator-inferred). Currently absorbed into `slot_prompt` prose without source-attribution.
- **Cross-channel coherence reasoning.** When the same campaign moves across touchpoints with different constraints, the reasoning for which visual anchors persist and which adapt. Currently operator-continuous; lives in `capture_reason` / `capture_notes` per-packet rather than as cross-packet reasoning.
- **Approval / governance chain.** Per-touchpoint approval rules (which touchpoints require which approvals). Currently captured only in `curation_pattern` enum at the curation seam, without per-touchpoint application rules surfaced upstream.

### What is currently prose-carried

Application-guidelines content distributes downstream into existing prose carriers: `constraint_rules` text (per-touchpoint operational rules); `bounded_creative_discretion` text (touchpoint-constraint bounding); `business_intent` text (which touchpoint the packet serves); `slot_prompt` text (per-role per-touchpoint composition instructions); `capture_reason` / `capture_notes` text (per-touchpoint judgment criteria reasoning). The prose carriers absorb the content; the source-attribution and reasoning chain do not surface structurally.

### What remains operator-continuous

The full chain from application-guidelines articulation through mode activation, per-touchpoint constraint setting, layer translation into packet and slot content, and curation criteria selection lives in operator continuity. At one-operator continuous-context scale, the operator carries the chain verbally; at any scale beyond one operator, the chain is structurally absent.

The dry-run-derived five trace dimensions (per PR #241) apply here:

- **Presence-of-inspection** — which application-guidelines documents the operator inspected (digital design system; brand book; touchpoint-specific Figma files; channel-specific guidelines). Currently absent.
- **Articulation-state** — whether application-guidelines content was rich, partial, sparse, generic, or absent per component. Currently absent.
- **Chain-position** — which fallback chain position resolved each piece of application-guidelines-derived content (stated / past-campaigns-inferred / library-segmentation-inferred / worked-example-default / operator-authored). Currently absent.
- **Derivation-basis** — for each piece of resolved application content, was it lifted from a stated rule, inferred from evidence (which evidence?), applied as worked-example default, or operator-authored (per PR #241's operator-authored-vs-inferred-not-stated distinction)? Currently absent.
- **Contradiction-resolution** — when application guidelines contradict photography style guide channel adaptations, or when stated touchpoint set diverges from past-campaigns evidence, how the contradiction was resolved. Currently absent; held candidate `contradiction_log` would cover.

### No carrier proposal

This artifact does not propose minimum-trace fields, structural carriers for application-guidelines content, or sub-classification of derivation-basis. It names the trace requirements at the current scale-legibility frontier and stops there. Per PR #241's posture, scale beyond operator continuity is treated as an architectural premise of the project (per grounding-note v58 + [`docs/architecture.md`](architecture.md)'s Scale-of-Operation reframe); the trace requirements name where legibility currently relies on operator continuity, not what would close the gap structurally.

## Earned vs Held

What this deepening clarifies at this evidence depth:

- **Application guidelines are the operationalization layer of the brand system.** Distinct from brand platform (definition), VIS (formal visual constants), photography style guide (photographic conventions), asset library (demonstrated execution), and past campaigns (historical operation), application guidelines articulate the *rules for moving brand-system content into operational use* across touchpoints. The deepening pattern that the four prior deepenings established generalizes to this category with operationalization-shaped content rather than definition-shaped, articulation-shaped, demonstration-shaped, or hybrid-shaped content.
- **The category's stage-load distribution across the four modes is asymmetric.** Heaviest in marketing mode (touchpoint-shaped at every slot); moderate-to-heavy in campaign mode (per-channel propagation + per-touchpoint review-context); moderate in collection mode (cross-slot consistency per touchpoint); lightest in SKU-driven mode (shared constraints across product-photography touchpoints).
- **Application guidelines pressure the mode-specific layer, packet layer, slot layer, and curation seam.** The brand-system layer receives a small share of application-guidelines content (cross-channel coherence principles at brand-wide scope); the category / product-class layer receives some content where brands articulate per-category application variation; candidate generation and governed-asset layers receive essentially none directly. The concentration at the middle layers reflects application guidelines' operational character — rules that bind production rather than constants that define identity.
- **Application guidelines as an intake site stresses scale-legibility at multiple layers simultaneously.** Mode activation, per-touchpoint constraint origin, cross-channel coherence reasoning, and approval / governance chain all currently rely on operator continuity.
